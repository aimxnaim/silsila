# The data model

## Why conventional HR data cannot answer the question

A typical HR record looks roughly like this:

```
employee_id | name          | job_title              | manager_id | department
E001        | Nurul Rashid  | Branch Ops Specialist  | M004       | Branch Ops
```

The job title is a **string on the person**. When it changes, the string is
overwritten. Three facts are destroyed by that single write:

1. What the title used to be.
2. Whether the new title is the same job or a different one.
3. That a specific human was sitting in the seat while it changed.

None of this is a bug. It is what a normalised state table is *for*. It is also
why the question "did this department grow, or did we rename things" costs a day
of manual archaeology.

## The shape Silsilah uses instead

Three objects and one relationship, defined in `src/domain/types.ts`.

```
Person ──────┐
             ├──< Assignment >──┐
Position ────┘                  │
    │                           │
    └──< Lineage >──> Position  │
                                │
             dated, sourced, confidence-scored
```

| Object | What it is | Exists independently of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Person` | A human being | Any job |
| `Position` | A seat in the organisation | Any human |
| `Assignment` | A dated link between the two | — |
| `LineageVerdict` | A dated, typed, evidenced link between two positions | — |

**`Assignment` is the join that makes the connection view possible.** It is the
intersection of the person's history and the organisation's history, and it is
the single table the brief's fourth requirement rests on.

**`LineageVerdict` is the part nobody else models.** It is what turns a sequence
of unrelated titles into a chain.

## Bitemporality, and why `source` is on every row

Every fact carries `source` and `confidence`.

That is not bookkeeping — it is what separates two kinds of time that HR data
constantly confuses:

- **Valid time** — when something was true in the world.
- **Record time** — when a system was told about it.

A redesignation effective 1 July 2022, recorded in a letter dated 14 June, and
entered into the HRIS in September, has three different dates attached to one
event. When the 2023 org chart and the transfer letter disagree, they are not
both wrong; they were written at different record times.

This is why conflicts are surfaced for a human to settle rather than resolved by
preferring the newer row. "Newer" is ambiguous when there are two clocks.

## The vocabulary

Six values, and they are the product's whole conceptual contribution:

| Relation | Meaning | What it implies about headcount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rename` | Same job, new wording | No growth |
| `redesignated` | Same job, new wording **and** a changed grade | No growth; changed cost |
| `split` | One position divided into several | Growth, from an existing seat |
| `merge` | Several consolidated into one | Contraction |
| `created` | Genuinely new, no predecessor | Real growth |
| `succeeded` | Predecessor exists, titles barely overlap | **Unknown — check by hand** |

`succeeded` is the most important of the six. It is the value the classifier
returns when it is *not confident*, and having somewhere honest to put those
cases is what stops the other five from being quietly wrong.

## Deriving versus guessing

The distinction the ingest stage is built around:

- **Deriving** is computing a value from records that exist, and saying so. A
  position with no creation date takes the date of its earliest assignment — and
  raises an `inferred` issue explaining that the seat may be older than the
  interface shows.
- **Guessing** is filling a gap to make the picture tidy. Silsilah does not do
  it. A missing reporting line stays missing and renders as hatch.

## The demonstration dataset

Generated by `scripts/generate-dataset.mjs` — 67 people, 78 positions, 80
assignment rows, Q1 2021 to Q2 2026.

It is kept as a script rather than a hand-typed blob so that every planted case
is visible as intent. Eight lineage stories are deliberately constructed
(a rename that becomes a redesignation, two splits, two merges, one low-overlap
succession, one genuine growth cohort) and exactly four data-quality defects are
planted, one per issue class.

The dataset is shaped like a large Malaysian bank so the demonstration reads as
familiar to a local audience. **Every person, position, date and document
reference in it was written for this project.** It contains no real employment
records and is not affiliated with or endorsed by any bank.
